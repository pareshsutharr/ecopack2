The physics in plain language

Paper fibres are hygroscopic: they absorb atmospheric water, soften, and slide under load. Stacking strength is the first casualty — a box that holds 400 kgf in the lab can quietly drop below 200 kgf mid-voyage. Above ~30% RH fibre weakening already begins; creep under sustained load means long-term usable strength is only 50–60% of the peak test value.

Where it bites hardest

Sea freight (30–45 day voyages, >90% RH container interiors), monsoon warehousing across Gujarat and coastal India, cold-chain condensation on freezer-to-dock cycles, and horticulture cold stores running near saturation.

The measurement that keeps everyone honest

The Cobb test (IS 4006 / TAPPI T441) measures how many grams of water a square metre of board absorbs in a timed interval — lower is better. IS 2771:2022 caps water absorption at 225 g/m² over 30 minutes for standard board; export-grade moisture-resistant work typically demands Cobb values of 30–40 g/m² or lower, because material above ~45 g/m² shows measurable dimensional change in ordinary warehouse humidity. We test per lot and share the numbers, so "moisture-resistant" on our documentation is a measurement, not a marketing word.

Engineering the fix

Three levers, applied together: coated or sized liners that hold Cobb at export grade (≤30–40 g/m²); board grades specified against humid-condition strength — not the lab number; and safety factors of 2–4× per ASTM D642 practice for stacking duration. This is the discipline behind our moisture-resistant flagship line.

What it means commercially

A few percent more board cost eliminates the transit-damage claims, rejected consignments and re-shipments that dwarf it. Commercially, corrugated boxes fall under HSN 4819, and GST on corrugated boxes was reduced from 12% to 5% with effect from 22 September 2025 — worth re-checking on old supplier quotations.
